PrintCard is one print in the grid view: thumbnail, identity, key numbers, status and tags.

**Anatomy.** Thumbnail (4:3, server-rendered PNG on the neutral `surface-2` ground with a 4px filament bar along its bottom edge; see the tint rule under SpoolSwatch; or a "Geen voorbeeld" fallback), selection checkbox top-left as 24px of ink in a `--pp-tap` target (2.1), name (600) with the missing-file icons behind it in the same line box (2.2), "klant / project" (`muted`), meta line (materiaal, print time with clock icon, grams), tags, then the price block on the left and the status badge on the right. Fluid width 220–320px; the grid uses `repeat(auto-fill, minmax(240px, 1fr))`, one column at 480px and below. **Deels betaald:** price block shows the remaining amount with the `pp-remaining` line, badge Deels betaald. **Niet factureren:** the price block shows "Kosten € x,xx" and the badge Niet factureren replaces the payment badge. **Verwijderd** (admin filter "Toon verwijderde"): thumbnail at 45% opacity, badge Verwijderd and a secondary small button "Herstellen" (one print; projects and customers have Herstellen in their header). **Gearchiveerd:** badge Gearchiveerd, thumbnail at 60% opacity. **Op factuur:** a locked print also shows the info badge "Op factuur {nummer}" in the tag row. **Mogelijk dubbel** appears only on the detail page.

**Tokens.** `surface`, `border`, `primary-tint-soft`/`primary` (selected), `primary-tint-strong` (live flash), `shadow-sm` (hover), `radius-lg`, `space-3`.

**States.** Default, hover, focus-visible (card link), selected, live update (1.6s flash, no layout shift), missing data, partial, niet factureren, archived, deleted.

**Accessibility.** The card is a link to the detail page with the name as accessible text; the checkbox and Herstellen are separate controls. Status is text plus icon.

**1.9.** (2026-10-07) *(the tint floor below is superseded by 2.0; it is kept because the measurements that killed it are measurements of this rule)* **Tint floor (GAP-071, proposed; needs your measurement).** The thumbnail's ground is the filament colour, which hides a model on a dark spool. Rule: the app computes the relative luminance **L** of the filament colour (WCAG formula) and sets `--mix` on the thumbnail: **L ≥ 0.18: `--mix: 100%`** (colour as is); below that **`--mix = max(40%, L / 0.18 × 100%)`**. The ground is then `color-mix(in srgb, var(--c1) var(--mix), var(--border-strong))`: a dark spool is blended towards `border-strong`, a mid blue-grey whose luminance is about 0.17 to 0.19 in **both** themes, so the floor is the same in light and dark and no new token is needed. The existing 28% white sheen stays, and a 1px inset `border-strong` ring at 50% opacity (`::after`) keeps a black tile separate from the card. A black spool is a grey ground (40% black), a dark purple is a dusky purple: still recognisable, no longer swallowing the model. Light filaments (L ≥ 0.18) are unchanged. `--mix` is a new allowed inline data property next to `--c1`. **To measure (owner):** the rendered model colour of a thumbnail (a dark grey model) and the ground colour of a black and of a dark purple tile, in the app after this change; I need the contrast ratio between them, aiming at 3:1 (WCAG 1.4.11 for a graphic). If a dark model on the 40% floor is below 3:1, raise the floor (50%) or lighten the model in the render, not the tint rule. **Subtitle (GAP-072, proposed: needs your confirmation; confirmed in 2.0).** The line under the name is "klant / project". When the project equals the print name (case and spaces ignored) it shows the klant alone ("Joey" instead of "Joey / Babybox locks"); a different project still shows "Joey / Kerstversiering 2026". Same rule in PrintRow's sub line and in the breadcrumb of the print detail ("Joey" alone; the heading already names the print). The share page has no klant and shows the date instead.

**2.0.** (2026-10-08) **The tint, decided (GAP-071, resolved — a direction, not another number).** Your table settles it: no `--mix` value clears 3:1 at both ends, and at 80% and 60% the result is worse than doing nothing. That is not a bad number, it is the wrong mechanism. **Contrast between an arbitrary user colour and any fixed ground cannot be guaranteed** — whatever ground we choose, some filament colour sits on top of it. Separation has to come from a shape cue, not from a colour pair, and the colour has to stop standing behind the thing it hides.

So: **the filament colour marks the object, never the ground behind it.**

- `.pp-thumb.tinted` is now the neutral `surface-2` ground in both themes — the same ground the share page took in 1.9 (GAP-079) for exactly this reason. The white sheen, the `color-mix()`, the inset ring and the data property **`--mix` are gone**; the app stops computing a luminance.
- The colour returns as a **4px bar along the bottom edge of the card thumbnail** (`.pp-print .pp-thumb.tinted::after`, from `--c1`, with a 1px `border-strong` hairline above it). It is a marker, never a ground: no render ever sits on it, so no 3:1 pair between colour and model exists to fail. The hairline gives the bar itself a 3:1 boundary on both grounds (`border-strong` on `surface-2`: 3.69 light, 3.32 dark).
- **The render carries its own outline.** Because the ground is now fixed, the one pair that matters is model against `surface-2`, and a black model in the dark theme would still vanish. The server-rendered PNG therefore outlines the model's silhouette with 1px of **`#6f7a96`** — the light `border-strong` value, baked in because an image cannot read tokens. Measured: 3.69:1 on light `surface-2`, 3.53:1 on dark `surface-2`, 4.29 and 3.99 on the two `surface` values. One ink works in both themes, which a theme-aware ground never could.
- In the list view the 40px thumbnail has **no** bar (`.pp-table .pp-thumb.sm.tinted::after{content:none}`): the row already carries the SpoolSwatch in its Materiaal cell, and 4px of 40 is a quarter of the picture. A model-strip button has no bar either.

**Breaking:** `--mix` leaves the contract; `.pp-thumb.tinted` and `.pp-plate.tinted` paint something else. A dark spool no longer tints anything, which is a visible change on every card.

On the card: the stretched link keeps the whole tile as the one target (GAP-070, unchanged); the checkbox chip is now visibly its own control (a `border-strong` hairline on the `surface` chip and the full `--pp-tap` hit area) and sits above the link; the tile never selects on a click, because a click that does two things is worse than one that is learned. `is-selected` (the `primary` border and tint) appears only after the checkbox is ticked, so nothing looks selected before anything is.

**2.3.** (2026-10-08) **The silhouette is outlined by the page, not by the renderer (GAP-071, the open half, resolved — and 2.0's sentence is withdrawn).**

The yes-or-no was: may the baked outline be drawn on **every** thumbnail the app serves, including the 79 the slicer made and we only pass through? **No.** But the problem is real for all 83, so the outline does not stay where 2.0 put it either — it moves.

- **2.0 wrote a rule for 2 prints.** "The server-rendered PNG therefore outlines the model's silhouette" is true of the **2** prints we render from STL; **79 of 83** take an image embedded in a G-code, 3MF or project file. I wrote the rule as though rendering were the normal path. It is the exception.
- **We do not rewrite a picture we did not make.** Compositing an outline into a slicer's own image means decoding, drawing over and re-encoding 79 files to change how they look on our ground — a bigger step than the sentence implied, and the owner was right to stop at it. It also keys the result to an alpha boundary the slicer chose, and it would have to be redone whenever the ink changes.
- **The edge is in the alpha channel, so the page can draw around it.** All 30 sampled thumbnails, the slicer's included, are PNGs with transparency. `.pp-art` on the picture draws four 1px `border-strong` drop-shadows from that alpha: **3.69:1 on light `surface-2`, 3.32:1 on dark** (WCAG 1.4.11 wants 3:1). Those are the *token's* two values, not one ink borrowed across both themes — a baked `#6f7a96` cleared both grounds (3.69 and 3.53) by luck, and luck is not a rule. A thumbnail that turns out to be fully opaque degrades to an outlined rectangle, which separates the picture from the card: the other half of the same complaint.
- **It also answers the measurement that made this urgent.** The renders are neutral grey, so against the real grounds the lightest pixel measures **1.16:1 on light `surface-2`** and the darkest **1.68:1 on dark** — a highlight lost in one theme and a shadow in the other, whatever the spool. A fixed ground and arbitrary picture content have no guaranteed contrast in *either* direction; that was 2.0's finding about filament colours and it is just as true of grey. Only an edge cue fixes it, and an edge cue that reads the theme fixes it in both.
- **Where it applies:** every thumbnail the app serves, at every size — the card, the 40px `pp-thumb sm` in PrintRow, the 76px square in the media strip when it ever carries a render, and the 48px thumbnail on the share page (which is why `.pp-share-thumb` becomes `position: relative`). One rule, one class, no server work.
- **`border-strong`'s usage text in `tokens.json` loses the "baked into the PNG" sentence.** No ink is baked anywhere now. The renderer's job is a picture of the model on a transparent ground; the page draws the outline. The 4px filament bar and its hairline (`.pp-thumb.tinted::after`) are unchanged and sit over the picture.

**Needs your measurement (this does not block building it).** The paint cost: the longest tile grid you can render (83 tiles, and the same page at 360px) with `.pp-art` on every thumbnail, against the same page without it — frame time or a scroll-jank figure, whichever your tooling gives honestly. Four chained drop-shadows per picture is cheap per element and I have not measured it at 83. If it regresses visibly, tell me and I will drop the grid to a single `drop-shadow(0 0 1px)` halo and keep the four-shadow ring on the detail page — do not make that trade yourself, because a blurred halo is partly transparent and no longer measures 3.69:1. And tell me whether any thumbnail in the library is **fully opaque**, so I know if the rectangle case is real or theoretical.
